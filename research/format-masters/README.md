## landing_page_short
Field: some_masters. Short landing pages have no hall of fame, no awards body and no long-running named controls, and I found no prize or hall of fame specific to this format. What exists is the conversion-copywriting and CRO community of 2009 to 2016, which published split tests with numbers. Two people in it wrote landing-page copy themselves, tested it in public with stated lifts, and are named by their peers: Joanna Wiebe and Michael Aagaard. Their evidence is real but has limits. Nearly all of it is self-reported or written up from their own data. Much of it covers single elements (a headline, a button, a form heading) or home pages, not whole one-screen pages. Most of the pages survive only as screenshots inside case studies. The third-best evidence comes from a research lab (MECLABS / MarketingExperiments under Flint McGlaughlin), not from a writer. The best-documented SaaS page wins on record (Conversion Rate Experts for Moz and Crazy Egg, and the Highrise long-form test) came from making pages longer, so "short wins" is not settled even for SaaS.

1. **Joanna Wiebe**: She is the only practitioner with several attributed, self-written, publicly tested rewrites of landing and home page copy: Crazy Egg +13% over a control Conversion Rate Experts had already optimized, Dressipi's button +123.9% clicks, SweatBlock +49% paid conversions at 99% confidence. Peers recognize her (Unbounce: 'the original conversion copywriter'). She has a manner no current master has: the customer's own sentence as the headline, the subheads taken from the top survey answers, and a first-person button that completes the headline. Caveat: all figures come from Copyhackers, the wins are mostly on home pages and single elements, and in this app her quoted headline must come from a verbatim on file.

The people most often called SMS experts (Firestone, Dimond) showed no SMS evidence on their own sites. So there are no recognized masters here. What can be studied are three team-written moves with evidence behind them: something already held for the reader, one sentence on what is at stake, and the news first.

1. **Katherine L. Milkman (lead author of the Behavior Change for Good flu-shot text megastudies)**: The strongest evidence for any text message: two peer-reviewed randomized field experiments (689,693 and 47,306 patients) that publish the winning texts word for word. The move is distinct and easy to copy: the brand, then the thing already held for him and where it is, and a second send. It is the only move here measured against no-text controls. The caveat: it is named for the research program, not a writer, because scientist teams wrote the texts, and it is honest only when something really is held for the reader (a cart, a saved report, a trial already set up).
   - source: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8833156/fullTextXML
   - source: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8157982/fullTextXML
   - source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/instance/8833156/bin/pnas.2115126119fig02.jpg

2. **M+R Strategic Services for Planned Parenthood (test reported by Johanna Levy)**: The only published split test I found where two texts differ by one move and the result is stated: adding one sentence on what is at stake raised revenue per recipient 50%, except on the deadline day, when they tied. It gives the app a clear way in for a text with no deal and no deadline: who the reader is to the cause, then one stakes clause, then the offer. The caveats: the result is agency-reported, the copy is team-written, it is a cause rather than a store, and the 280-character winner has to shrink to fit the app's 240-character limit.
   - source: https://www.mrss.com/?p=13841

3. **Obama for America 2008 new media team (the vice-presidential announcement text)**: The weakest pick, listed for its way in: news first, as a past-tense fact from a sender the reader knows by first name, with the time, the place and one action. Third-party reach is on record (Nielsen: 2.9 million recipients), but there is no response figure and the writer is not named. Use it as the 'news' way in, not as a master.
   - source: https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/text-message-announcing-selection-senator-joseph-biden-the-2008-democratic-vice
   - source: https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2008/obamas-text-message-reaches-29-million-and-makes-history/
   - source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama_2008_presidential_campaign

Also considered: Lillie Sun (Three Ships Beauty); Dean Jackson; Attentive research team (25-billion-message analysis; article by Angela Rollins); Ezra Firestone; Chase Dimond

## cold_email
Field: some practitioners, little independent data. Most reply-rate figures come from tool vendors.

1. **Aaron Ross**: Built Salesforce's outbound team and its "Cold Calling 2.0" process, credited with adding about $100M in recurring revenue (secondary and promotional sources). His move is the short referral email to a senior person asking only who handles the area. Readable in Predictable Revenue (2011).
   - source: https://resources.rework.com/libraries/leadership-legends/aaron-ross-leadership
   - source: https://anymailfinder.com/blog/predictable-revenue
2. **Jon Buchan (Charm Offensive)**: The most distinct manner in the field: humor and self-deprecation that admits it is a cold email ("You've never heard of me. (Hi, I'm Jon). I got your details from a list."). Reported meetings with Red Bull and Pepsi are anecdotal. Already quoted in docs/03 §5.21.
   - source: https://selzy.com/en/blog/jon-buchan-interview
   - source: https://getshitdone.beehiiv.com/p/how-a-drunk-cold-email-changed-my-life
3. **Alex Berman**: His observation, outcome-without-pain, one-word-answer structure is the spec's backbone already (docs/03 §5.21). No published figures. Alternates: Josh Braun ("Poke the Bear" neutral questions; no figures) and Bryan Kreuzberger (Breakthrough Email; self-reported 90% response and $20M client business).
   - source: https://mixergy.com/course-cheat-sheet-cold-email/

## pricing_page
Field: thin. No named writer is known for pricing pages; the evidence is agency and lab tests.

1. **Conversion Rate Experts (Karl Blanks, Ben Jesson)**: Wrote and tested pricing pages with stated lifts: Smart Insights +157% from anchoring against the expensive alternative, Earth Class Mail +57% from restating the value argument above the price (already in docs/03 §5.22).
2. **Joanna Wiebe (Copyhackers)**: Value headline over the price ("Cheaper than downtime."), plans named "Best for [persona]", FAQ headings written as the objection. Same person as the landing page pick.
3. **37signals (Jason Fried)**: Published their own Highrise and Basecamp sign-up and pricing tests (long form +37.5% net signups; a later shorter page +47% paid signups). Strategist-writer, company-reported.

## podcast_read
Field: some masters, mostly from radio. Show-level sales data exists; read-level data mostly does not.

1. **Paul Harvey**: The benchmark live read. Sponsors reportedly paid about $1M a year for his reads, and he refused products he had not used himself. His read slid out of the story so listeners missed the switch. No sales figures.
   - source: https://www.wfae.org/united-states-world/2009-03-01/radio-fans-mourn-paul-harvey
2. **Arthur Godfrey**: Invented the ad-libbed, kidding-the-sponsor host read in the 1940s and 50s. An advertiser reported +30% New York sales against +12% nationally (Variety, 1946, self-reported).
   - source: https://talkers.com/greatest/15tGodfrey.htm
   - source: https://lantern.mediahist.org/catalog/variety162-1946-05_0312
3. **Bill Burr (Monday Morning Podcast)**: Ranked first among comedy podcasts on Oxford Road's ORBIT index, built from $1.6B+ of campaign data on acquisition cost and return on ad spend (Nov 19, 2025). Caveat: the ranking measures the show, not his reads alone. Alternate: Oxford Road (Dan Granger) as the strategist for the read structure.
   - source: https://soundsprofitable.com/?p=10926
