## Notes before you publish

1. **Fill in the real kit contents.** The parts list above is a typical setup. Don't promise sensors or projects the kit doesn't include, because parents will check, and so will chargebacks.
2. **Only show a crossed-out price if it's real.** If the kit isn't sold for more elsewhere, delete that line. It's illegal in many places otherwise, and the $199 offer works fine without it.
3. **Keep the "one-time" claim true.** If you'll sell the kit at $199 later (say, in a post-camp email), soften it to "best price we offer."
4. **Add real proof if you have it.** One genuine parent quote or a photo of a kid with a finished robot, placed right above the first button, usually beats more copy. Don't make up testimonials.
5. **Test a downsell.** For people who click "No thanks," a $99 "Starter Kit" (fewer sensors, same controller) often brings back a good share of them.
