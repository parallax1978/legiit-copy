**Headline:**
# This Summer, Your Kid Stops Playing Games and Starts Building Them

**Subhead:**
*A hands-on coding camp for ages 9–14 where every camper goes home with a game, app, or website they made themselves.*

---

Ask your kid what they want to be when they grow up, and there's a good chance the answer involves a screen. YouTuber. Game designer. The person who builds the next Minecraft.

But right now, most of that screen time is spent consuming: scrolling, watching, tapping through things someone else made. Once school lets out, that adds up to hours a day.

What if this summer went differently?

At [Camp Name], kids ages 9 to 14 swap passive screen time for something they'll be proud of. They learn to code by building projects they actually care about: a platformer game starring their own characters, an app that fixes a small problem in their life, or a website about whatever they're obsessed with this month.

There are no dry worksheets and no lectures on syntax. Campers learn by making things, breaking them, and fixing them. Our instructors know how to keep a 10-year-old engaged and a 14-year-old challenged in the same room.

On the last day, they show off what they made. Parents come to a demo where every camper presents their project. (Fair warning: you'll probably be asked to play their game. More than once.)

The skills last, too. Coding teaches kids to break a big problem into small steps, stay patient when something doesn't work, and try again. Those habits help in math class, at the science fair, and long after camp ends.

We keep groups small so every camper gets real attention from an instructor. Tuition is $899 and covers all instruction and materials [plus snacks/lunch — confirm]. No coding experience is needed, and [laptops are provided — confirm].
