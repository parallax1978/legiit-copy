## Opening

When someone searches "car accident lawyer near me" or "divorce attorney [city]," Google usually shows three firms in a map before any regular website results.

Those three firms get the calls. The firms listed below them often don't.

What decides who gets those three spots isn't always the size of the firm, how long it has been around, or how good its lawyers are. Often it's whether the firm's Google Business Profile is complete, accurate, in the right categories, and active.

Most law firm profiles we audit have at least one of these problems:

- **Wrong or missing primary category.** "Law Firm" when it should be "Personal Injury Attorney"
- **Name, address, and phone that don't match** across the web, which weakens Google's trust in the listing
- **No services, no practice-area descriptions, no Q&A.** That leaves empty space for competitors to fill
- **Old or stock photos**, or no photos at all
- **No posting activity** in months
- **Reviews with no responses**, or no system for asking for reviews that fits bar rules

Each of these is fixable. You just need someone to do it properly.

## Notes before you publish

- **Keep the scarcity true.** If the $1,500 price is ever offered again later, change "today only / only here" to something accurate, like "available to new strategy-call clients." Fake scarcity hurts trust, and in some places it's an FTC issue.
- **Anchor price:** only show a crossed-out "regular price" if you actually sell the service at that price.
- **Social proof:** the page has no testimonials because I didn't want to invent any. If you have a real law firm result (for example, "went from #9 to #2 in the map pack for 'DUI lawyer Tampa'"), put it right under the offer section. That's usually the single biggest boost to conversions.
- **Bar compliance:** the review and description language deliberately avoids incentives and outcome promises, since many state bars restrict both. Keep it that way if you edit.
